Learning Transferable Visual Models From Natural Language Supervision
Alec Radford, Jong Wook Kim, Chris Hallacy, Aditya Ramesh, Gabriel Goh, Sandhini Agarwal, Girish Sastry, Amanda Askell, Pamela Mishkin, Jack Clark, Gretchen Krueger, Ilya Sutskever
2021
arXiv:2103.00020

Abstract
CLIP trains image and text encoders jointly with a contrastive objective so visual representations align with natural language descriptions without task-specific class labels at scale.

Methodology
The approach collects image-text pairs from the public Internet and learns dual encoders that map images and text into a shared embedding space. Training maximizes similarity of correct pairings while pushing mismatched pairings apart.

Datasets training
Training uses a large proprietary dataset of 400 million image-text pairs collected from public web sources. Evaluation uses zero-shot transfer to ImageNet classification, MS-COCO retrieval-style tasks, and robustness stress tests.

Experiments
The paper studies model scale (ResNet and Transformer vision backbones), batch size effects for contrastive learning, and prompt engineering for zero-shot classifiers built from natural language templates.

Results
CLIP demonstrates strong zero-shot performance on ImageNet and competitive results on several vision benchmarks when adapted linearly, motivating later vision-language systems.
