Latent ODEs for Irregularly-Sampled Time Series
Yulia Rubanova, Ricky T. Q. Chen, David Duvenaud
2019
arXiv:1907.03907

Abstract
Neural ordinary differential equation (ODE) models represent latent state dynamics with a continuous-time neural vector field, enabling sequence modeling when observations arrive at irregular timestamps.

Methodology
An ODE solver integrates latent dynamics between observations; an RNN or recognition network produces an approximate posterior over initial states. For partially observed systems, latent ODE combines variational inference with adaptive-step solvers.

Datasets
Experiments include synthetic ODE-driven data and physiological time series derived from public healthcare-style benchmarks where sampling is non-uniform. Rotating MNIST sequences are used as a controlled video-like benchmark with irregular frames.

Experiments
The paper reports likelihood and imputation metrics, emphasizing stability across adaptive solvers and trade-offs between expressivity and training time.

Results
Latent ODE models outperform naive discretization approaches when timestamps are irregular, at additional computational cost from numerical integration.
