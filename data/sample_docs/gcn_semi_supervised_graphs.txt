Semi-Supervised Classification with Graph Convolutional Networks
Thomas N. Kipf, Max Welling
2017
arXiv:1609.02907

Abstract
Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) perform semi-supervised node classification by stacking localized spectral graph convolutions that aggregate neighbor representations.

Methodology
Each layer applies a degree-normalized adjacency operator followed by linear transformation and ReLU. Only a small fraction of node labels are observed during training, while the model propagates supervision across edges.

Datasets
Experiments focus on citation network benchmarks: Cora, Citeseer, and Pubmed. Nodes represent documents, edges represent citation links, and features are sparse bag-of-words vectors.

Experiments
The paper reports classification accuracy with varying label rates and compares against label propagation and earlier graph embedding baselines.

Results
GCNs achieve competitive accuracy on Cora with clean training curves, and motivate deeper graph neural architectures for relational data.
