IEEE-CIS Fraud Detection: Tabular Learning Competition Data and Practical Constraints
IEEE Computational Intelligence Society Competition Team (summarized technical brief)
2019

Abstract
The IEEE-CIS fraud detection challenge provides high-dimensional tabular transaction features for binary fraud classification under severe class imbalance and anonymized fields, making it a standard stress test for gradient boosting and calibration methods.

Methodology
Participants typically employ tree ensembles with monotonic constraints in some variants, feature engineering on aggregations across identities and transaction time windows, and probability calibration or cost-sensitive thresholds.

Datasets
The primary dataset is the IEEE-CIS fraud transaction table distributed via Kaggle, including continuous and categorical attributes with masked semantics. Auxiliary identity tables link transactions to entities.

Experiments
Evaluation emphasizes area under ROC (AUC) and business-aligned metrics such as recall at fixed false positive rates. Many solutions combine stratified cross-validation and out-of-fold stacking.

Results
Strong baselines rely on XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost ensembles with careful treatment of categoricals and leakage checks.
